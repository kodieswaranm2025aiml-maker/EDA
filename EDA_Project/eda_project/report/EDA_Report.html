Introduction & Dataset Overview

This report presents a comprehensive Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) of an Employee Performance and Salary dataset containing 500 records across 10 features. Key Findings
  • 📈
    Experience is the Strongest Salary Predictor
    Pearson correlation of r = 0.786 between Experience and Salary indicates that each additional year of experience is associated with approximately ₹1,500 increase in annual salary.
  • 🎓
    Education Level Moderately Impacts Salary
    PhD holders earn on average ₹24,000–₹32,000 more than High School graduates. Education correlates with salary at r = 0.402, confirming a meaningful but secondary effect compared to experience.
  • 🏆
    Engineering Department Leads in Both Salary and Performance
    Engineering employees earn an average of ₹80,096 — 29% above the HR/Sales departments — and also score highest in average performance (3.50/5.00).
  • 😊
    Performance and Satisfaction Are Strongly Linked
    r = 0.788 correlation between Performance Score and Job Satisfaction suggests that high performers are significantly more satisfied with their jobs, pointing to a virtuous cycle of engagement.
  • ⚖️
    Gender Salary Gap Is Minimal
    Mean salaries for Male (≈₹70,100) and Female (≈₹68,900) employees are close, suggesting no significant gender-based pay disparity in this dataset after controlling for other factors.
  • 🗺️
    Mumbai and Delhi Concentrate 50% of the Workforce
    The workforce is geographically concentrated, with Mumbai (~25%) and Delhi (~25%) together accounting for half of all employees. Bangalore follows at ~20%.

Conclusion & Recommendations

Analytical Summary

This EDA reveals that experience and education are the two most influential drivers of employee salary, with experience carrying nearly twice the predictive weight. The strong link between performance scores and job satisfaction points to a positive reinforcement loop that HR teams can leverage. Engineering stands out as the highest-performing and highest-compensated department.


Recommendations: Organizations should invest in skill-development programs to accelerate functional experience, particularly for junior employees. The positive performance–satisfaction relationship suggests that recognizing high performers with non-monetary rewards can further boost satisfaction scores. Salary benchmarking across cities should be reviewed, as geographic concentration in Mumbai and Delhi may create cost-of-living disparities.
